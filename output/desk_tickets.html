Reflection

1. How the agents did

Ticket 101

The agents got the facts right. Size S of CC-TEE-WHITE is at 0, and invoice 501 is an overdue $840. No agent ever called pay, so checking only dropped from $3,400 to $2,560 once the shop owner approved the payment. What went wrong was after that. Customer service sent inventory back to the shelf a second time for no reason, and then the route marked the ticket resolved even though the note said to leave it open.

Ticket 102

The lease facts were right. The rent is $2,400, due 2026-09-02, and it isn't overdue as of the shop's today. Accounting read the live drawer, saw $2,560, which still covers the rent, and after the approval checking sat at $160. The weak spot is how many times the work went in circles. The boss, facilities, accounting, and customer service kept passing the same lease around. A second ask to accounting got refused, and two landlord drafts got written. That loop is what blew the token cap on the earlier attempts.

Ticket 103

This was the cleanest split of the three. Inventory said there were 8 on hand against the 20 the club wanted. Accounting held the price at the $22 unit cost floor and didn't make up a discount. No money moved, so checking stayed at $160. The miss is the resolved stamp. The agent's note says the club still has to name a price, and the route resolved the ticket anyway.

2. Plan versus what happened

Ticket 101

The main path matched my plan. Inventory went first, then accounting, then customer service. Facilities stayed out, and no agent called pay. Two things weren't in the plan. Inventory got a second lap, and the route resolved the ticket before the $840 approval happened.

Ticket 102

The first call matched, since facilities went first, and inventory stayed out. My plan said the boss wouldn't call accounting directly, but the boss did. My plan also assumed checking was still $3,400. The run correctly saw $2,560, because the tee bill from 101 had already been paid.

Ticket 103

The order matched, and so did the $22 floor. My plan said Bulldog Print Co was still blocked by invoice 501, but the run found them unblocked because the $840 had already posted. My plan said to leave the ticket open, and the agent's note said the same thing. The route resolved it anyway, with no approval after.

Ticket 102 is the obvious one. It comes down to one lease row and one cash row. A single agent would have gotten the same $2,400 and the same $2,560 coverage without the refused handoff or the second draft. Ticket 101 didn't need check_stock twice. Ticket 103 didn't need read_pricing twice, and it didn't need a cash read at all, since $0 was leaving the drawer. The one piece I'd keep separate is the approve button. Agents still shouldn't call pay, and the cash tab only moves when the two signatures happen.
